.What is {app-name} and why does it exist?
****

{app-name} is a platform that helps students in two ways:

* If you want to give turoring for whatever reason, you can easily share your
  availability with your entire school community;
* If you want tutoring, there will be a list of other students in your school
  community who, as students, know what you are going through, and may be able
  to help you in a way that the teachers cannot.

This facilitates the act of giving and receiving help, while strengthening the
school community.

=== Purpose

For me, at first, this project was for me to maybe make some money to pay for
my lunch at school, and also to add things to my curriculum vitæ, and if I
could show that I was using this project IRL on a daily basis, I think that
would give it a lot of value.

But this project gives a lot more than that, and it doesn't just have to
benefit me.

[cols="1,1"]
|===
|Problem |Solution

|A message in a WhatsApp group chat advertising your tutoring service doesn't
look to professional. Or a paper on a wall.
|If it was displayed on a website dedicated to promoting tutoring services.
That looks more formal and professional.

|I also don't know who you are looking at your phone number.
|Use your school e-mail as your primary form of identity. Knowing that you are
also a student, makes me feel more comfortable.

|Are you even a student at my school?
|If you have a school e-mail, if you are not now, at least you were before.

|This teacher sucks, I can't understand anything he's is trying to teach in
class.
|I don't think it's the teacher's problem. I don't think it's your problem
either. Teachers have different ways of teaching and evaluating students.
Different students have different ways of learning better. Sometimes your
classmate can explain you in a different way, that is easier for you to
understand.

|I'm getting good grades, but I see that many of my classmates are struggling.
Sometimes they ask me for my help, but it's hard to help them all at once.
|{app-name} can help you manage your schedule.

|I think I'm capable of tutoring, but I feel that it's hard to start without
clients. How acn I advertise my services to potential clients?
|Create and configure a tutor profile. When other students search for help in a
subject you can help them with, you'll be displayed to them.

|I don't have money to pay for outside help with my studies.
|Other students will probably understand and even share the same situation. You
may find a student who can help you on a subject in exchange for your help in
another. No money involved, just sharing and exchanging wisdom.

=== Understanding

Let's deconstruct the project's slogan if I can call it that.

[quote]
{description}

I really need to explain the term *S2S*, the concept of student-to-student
comes from the concept of https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer[P2P] from
computing and networking where you have peers that in our case are the
students, and they can be both the client (the one who learns) and the server
(the one who teaches). All peers are equipotent in their network.

But it's not all a bed of roses, there're still routing attacks and corrupted
data and malware, because there can be students with bad intentions: Saying
they know about something when they don't, lying about their GPA, teaching you
the wrong stuff. That's why an instance of this project needs to have a good
ToS and CoC, and punish the bad nodes accordingly. Another way to combat this
is to have a grading system for both students and tutors, so that you can have
an idea of what you are signing up for, and as a tutor you can receive
constructive criticism to improve your service.

The more students both learn and teach the better for everyone.

Now "`{description}`" probably makes perfect sense.

Note that all this has been done basically without the actual involvement of
the school, if the school doesn't provide the students with school e-mails that
offer SSO integration, there will be workarounds, don't worry, that's a problem
for me, the developer, to figure out.

The tools are in the hands of the students, literally. Not even I need to be
involved in your school's {app-name} community network. This project is pushing
for decentralization and self-hosting, and there are ways for the actual school
to be involved in that, without the students losing their power.

This couldn't be an education-friendly project without being under an
education-friendly software license. If I were to make this software
proprietary, I would be restricting knowledge. Not very educational, is it?

Right now, you can study how this software was made, maybe modify something for
your use case, run your modified copy and share it with the world the same way
I'm sharing this copy. That is freedom. That is beautiful.

You can also make sure that I don't do anything weird with your information for
example. Most of the software that you use today doesn't give you that freedom
I'm sure.

And it's sad how the schools I've been to do not seem to have this perspective.
I'm here to change that as well.

Not to say that Windows and Microsoft Office licenses are a waste of money when
you could very well use be using a Linux or BSD distribution - I'm mentioning
https://caixamagica.pt/pt/linux-caixa-magica/[Caixa Mágica], because it might
be my first impression of FOSS, my ICT teacher sometimes made us use it in his
classes instead of the Windows they had, I was between 6 and 10 years old - and
LibreOffice. That money could be used in better places like reducing the price
of meals in the university cafeteria for everyone in the school community.

And then the students create a dependency on this kind of "`harmful`" software
and something that was free when they were in school, isn't anymore.

If feel that sometimes there's an idea that the more you pay for something, the
better the quality is, and if something is given for free, then there's no
quality once however in that product. That's a fallacy.

I'm also currently in another exchange program, in Niterói, Rio de Janeiro,
Brasil (first sesmeter of 2025), doing an
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human%E2%80%93computer_interaction[HCI] course.

As a software developer, I sometimes do things the way that suits me best,
forgetting that I'm just one of many people using the software. You have to be
able to look at the software like any other kind of user. It's obvious that
I'll know how to use my product, I made it, but will the people I made the
software for have the same ease?

I'll try my best to really learn and put into practice the things I get from
this classrom.

And because the school community isn't just made up of students, and teachers,
hopefully everyone including every type of staff, employee can be a part of
this and hopefully you can find a cleaning janitor who can teach you how to
clean your dirty ass room, so that your mom doesn't have to do it next time.

== About {app-name}

{app-name} is an S2S peer tutoring management platform, built for students, by
students. Focusing on a11y and inclusivity, straightforward ease of use,
convenience but also decentralization and freedom.

== Philosophy

Education should not be a privilege, nor only for the elite and

== Key Features

* Easy sign-up using the school e-mail;
* Simple way to search for a tutor from your school that fits your need;
* Easy to setup a tutor profile where:
** You can tell about yourself (your degree, study areas you can help with,
   tutoring prices, link to your curriculum vitæ, locations of tutoring, ...;
** Specify when you are unavailable to do tutoring, students will not be able
   to ask for help during that time;
** You can pause the tutoring status whenever you want;
* Students and tutors review systems;
* Easy way to visualize how's your schedule.

=== Installation & Build instructions

==== Get the source code

Like I said, there's various ways to do this, my recommendation is to use git
and the url of the repository you want to start with:

.Cloning this repository
[,console]
----
git clone https://github.com/torres-engineer/splikan.git
----

==== Install project dependencies

This will download external code used by this project:

.Install dependencies
[,console]
----
deno install
----

==== Build the project

If everything was done right running this on the console should cause no problem:

.Build
[,console]
----
deno task build
----

=== Running the app

.Start a development server
[,console]
----
deno task dev -- --open
----

.Start a server with your build output
[,console]
----
deno task start
----
